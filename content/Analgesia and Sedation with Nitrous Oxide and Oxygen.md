# Analgesia
- Altered state of consciousness
- fear and apprehension are eliminated
- interpretation of painful stimuli is altered
- colorless gas
- slightly sweet
- non-irritating

# Tanks
NO2: 99.5% purity, blue color (int'l code); compressed at 750-850 psi; there's some liquid remaining; regulator always appears full because as the liquid goes down, the air goes up
medical oxygen: 100% purity: green; compressed at 2200 psi

# Effects
- analgesic/anti-anxiety agents causing CNS depression
- euphoria
- minimal respiratory effects

## Analgesic Effects:
- Endogenous neuronal nrelease of opioid peptides with activation of opioid receptors and GABA-A receptors in noradrenergic pathways that modulate nociceptive processes at the spinal level

## Pharmacokinetics
- High blood solubility coefficient: 0.47
- Does not bind to hemoglobin
- Highly vascularized tissues (brain) quickly admit N2O reaching equilibrium
- not metabolized in the body
- eliminated mainly by alveolar route (3 min).
- about 1% eliminated more slowly (24 hrs) via lungs and skin
- least toxic inhalation anesthetic
- no known allergic reactions

## Concentration effects
- N2O rapidly disappears from teh alveoli
- It promotes a repeat delivery of the anesthetic, producing high concentrations of the gas

## Fink Diffusion Hypoxia
- adverse effect that appears in anesthetic recovery
- occurs upon discontinuation of N2O administration.
- Creates an alveolar-to-circuit gradient, allowing N2O to leave the bloodstream into alveoli, diluting oxygen concentration

## Hypoxia by two mechanisms
1. Displacing and diluting O2 in the alveolar-capillary membrane
2. Diluting CO2, decreasing respiratory drive
	1. Clinical presentation: "*hangover effect*"
	2. Prevented by administering 100% O2 for 3-5 minutes.

Dose-Resopnse Relationship of Nitrous Oxide in Healthy Individuals
- 20%: Analgesia equivalent to *15 mg morphine*
- 40%: Inability to cooperate
- 60%: Almost complete amnesia
- 80%: Unconsciousness
- 100%: No movement with surgical stimulation

80-100 will never be reached because of how we set up the equipment

## Cardiovascular Effects
- 40% N2O - 60% O2
- Reduces cardiac output by 20%
- Increases peripheral vascular resistance by 20%
- No effect on blood pressure
- Advantageous in patient with cerebrovascular disorders

## CNS Effects
- in dental concentrations, it **does not depress the respiratory or vasomotor centers**.
- It potentiates the action of other CNS depressants (opiates and barbiturates).
- It has an amnesiac effect (shortens the perception of time).

## Respiratory Effects
- Increases respiratory rate with slight decrease in pulmonary ventilation and CO2 response.
- Concentrations above 75% may cause hypoxia, pulmonary edema, and heart failure.

## Adverse effects
- Rare overall
- Nausea and vomiting most common (0.5-1.2%)
	- Associated with prolonged administration, fluctuating gas levels, poor titration, or heavy meals before administration

## Other Effects
- Irreversible oxidation of Vitamin B12 leading to anemia (days of exposure)
- Not carcinogenic
- **Prolonged exposure** (not applicable to dental use) associated with 
	- Kidney and liver disease
	- Neurological disorders
	- Infertility and reproductive difficulty
	- Bone marrow suppression
	- Exposure to high concentrations of nitrous oxide as a potential cause of female fertility disorders

## Safety LImits
- NIOSH limit of 25 ppm
- Dental equipment is exposed 12 times more
- Elevated areas with high O2 demand should not exceed 60% N2O
- The scavenger reaches 36.6 ppm
- Combined with the ejector, it's lower than 25 ppm

## Occupational hazards
- Women exposed to untreated gas for more than 6 hours per week have a pregnancy rate of less than 60% per cycle.
- Of women with high exposures without treatment, 42% took more than a year to become pregnant, compared to 11% of unexposed women.

# 3 Stages of analgesia with N2O-O2
(Langa and Parbrook)
- Plane 1: Moderate sedation and relative analgesia
- Plane 2: Dissociated sedation and relative analgesia
- Plane 3: Total analgesia

## Plane 1
- Concentrations of 5%-25% N2O and 95%-75% O2
- Symptoms
	- Tingling sensation in hands, tongue, and lips
	- Relaxation, increased pain threshold, and decreased fear.
	- Heaviness in arms and legs
- Signs
	- Patient responds correctly to questions
	- Pupillary reflexes maintained
	- Perioral muscle relaxation
	- Increased pain threshold
	- Decreased fear

## Plane 2
Concentration sof 20-55% NwO and 80%-45% O2
**Symptoms**
- More poronounced sensation fo warmth and numbness.
- Feelings of dissociation, euphoria, and talkativeness.
- Dreaminess and fantasies
- Increased paresthesia in the arms and legs
*Signs*
- Dreamy, distant gaze
- Decreased blinking
- The patient cooperates with verbal commands
- Slight amnesia of the treatment
- With greater depth of sedation and analgesia the patient responds to questions but finds it difficult
## Plane 3
Concentrations of 50-70% N2O and 50%-30% O2
**Symptoms**
- All of the above symptoms
- Almost complete analgesia
- Loss of consciousness, ranging from 60% to 45%-50% in some cases
- Tendency to dream and profound amnesia
*Signs*
- The patient cannot keep their mouth open
- Light anesthesia may occur within a few minutes
- Deep drowsiness

 *Children readily accept the use of Nitrous oxide because of the color and smell*

## Advantages of NO2
- Rapid onset (inhalation 2-3 min vs oral 30 min, IM 10-15 min, IV 1min)
- Peak sedation can be maintained as needed
- Sedation depth easily adjustable at any time
- Time of sedation is controllable
- Immediate recovery (3-5 min vs. 2-3 hrs for oral/rectal/IM)
## Disadvantages
- Cost of equipment and gases
- Equipment requires space
- Requires minimal patient cooperation
- Requires professional training for safe use

## Complications
1. excessive sweating
	1. facial flushing
	2. solution: decrease N2O by 5%
2. Behavioral problems
	1. Authoritarian patients
	2. slow induction
	3. solution: decrease N2O by 5-10% 
3. Tremor
	1. N2O causes peripheral vasodilation, leading to a physiological vasoconstrictive reflex response that can lead to tremors
	2. Usually self-limiting
	3. May treat symptomatically
		1. Management: 
			1. reassure patient that it's a common, mild effect
			2. warm the patient (blanket) 
			3. reduce nitrous oxide concentration
			4. discontinue N2O if tremors don't improve
4. Nausea and vomiting
	1. less than 0.3% associated with oversaturation

## Protocols for Administration Rapid Induction
- Semi-supine position of the patient
- Determine liters per minute
- Placement and adjustment of nasal mask (sizes S, M, L).
- Start with 100% O2, then introduce 40% N2O. 
- Adjust concentration by 5% every 2 min as needed
- Monitor visually at each increment
- Determine ideal % for the patient
- Begin dental treatment
	- Write time dental treatment starts
- Final oxygenation at 100% for 3-5 min after the procedure
- Post-treatment observation and record keeping

## Stepwise Titration
- Patient in supine position
- Operator behind the patient
- "Tell-show-do" technique
- Flow: 4 L/min O2 in children, 6-7 L/min adults
- Induction: reach 40% N2O - 60% O2
- 100% O2 for 4-5 min after discontinuation
- Record sedation data
	- probably next appointment, you may need to inc. concentration of N2O

# Monitoring
Oximeter: Monitor O2 saturation in hemoglobin

## Oximeter
- detection of relative differences in light absorption (red and ultrared) of saturated and unsaturated hemoglobin during arterial pulsation
- Placement: finger ankle ear lobe
False alarms:
- patient movement
- hypothermia
- lighting
- abnormal hemoglobins
- blood flow changes
*moderate hypoxemia*: O2 saturation <95%

## Tips for good management
- voice control and calm environment
- appropriately sized mask
- predictable symptoms
- maintain physical contact and reassurance
- never leave patient alone
- limit patient talking
- always thank the patient for cooperation
