# TMJ
- Type of joint: ginglymoarthrodial joint, which is a compound joint
- By definition, a compound joint requires 3 bones but there is only 2 bones in the TMJ; the articular disc functions as the 3rd bone in the TMJ.

Why? Exhibits 2 types of movement
- ginglymoid = hinging/rotational movement
- arthrodial = gliding/translational movement

TMJ formed by: 
1. **C**ondyle (Mandible)
2. **M**andibular Fossa (Temporal bone) (i.e. glenoid fossa/articular fossa) e
3. **A**rticular disc - Separates the **C** and **M**

## Articular Disc
- composition: dense fibrous CT, relatively avascular and a 
- Shape: 
	- Sagitally, can be divided into 3 regions according to thickness
		- Intermediate zone:  the thinnest part of the disc, located centrally
		- Anterior & Posterior Regions are thicker
		- Posterior region is the thickest
	- Anterior View: disk is thicker medially than laterally
	- Disc is flexible and adapatable but the morphology is not altered during function; only if there are destructive forces or joint pathology.

## Retrodiscal Tissue
- anteriorly, attaches to the posterior region of the  **Articular Disc 
- highly vascularized and innervated
-  *superior retrodiscal lamina*
	- attaches the posterior aspect of the articular **disc to** the **tympanic plate**
	- contains many elastic fibers
- *inferior retrodiscal lamina*
	- attaches the inferior border of the posterior edge of the **disc to** the posterior margin of the articular surface of the **condyle**
	- contains many collagenous fibers
- posterior aspect
	- attached to a large venous plexus, which fills with blood as the condyle moves forward

## Capsular Ligament
- surrounds most of the joint
- attached to the superior and inferior regions and medial and lateral regions of the centrally-located disc
- Attached superiorly to the anterior margin of the articular surface of the temporal bone by collagenous fibers
- Attached inferiorly to the anterior margin of the articular surface of the condyle by collagenous fibers
- The corresponding attachments form superior and inferior cavities
- the inner surface of the cavities are formed by specialized endothelial cells that form a synovial lining.
- the **synovial fringe**, located at the anterior border of the retrodiscal tissues, and the specialized endothelial cells produce synovial fluid that fills the superior and inferior cavities.
- thus, the TMJ is considered a synovial joint
- Synovial fluid
	- 1)  Serves as a medium for metabolic requirements between the vessels of the capsule and the articular tissues, since the articular surfaces of the TMJ are nonvascular.
	- 2) lubrication%%  %%

#### 4 Histologic Layers/zones
from superficial to deep:
1) Articular zone
	1) made of dense fibrous CT 
		1) this is opposed to most synovial joints, where the articular zone is made of hyaline cartilage
2) Proliferative zone
	1) contents: undifferentiated mesenchymal tissue, which stimulated to differentiate and proliferate into articular cartilage during loading.
3) fibrocartilaginous zone
	1) contents: collagen fibrils arranged in bundles in a crossing pattern in random orientation to resist compressive and lateral forces
4) calcified cartilage zone
	1) contents: chondrocytes and chondroblasts; the dead chondrocytes turn into bone cells within the medullary cavity
	2) the scaffolding provided by the extracellular matrix provides an active site for remodeling activity during endosteal bone growth
