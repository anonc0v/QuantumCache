# Skull
# TMJ
- Type of joint: ginglymoarthrodial joint, which is a compound joint
- By definition, a compound joint requires 3 bones but there is only 2 bones in the TMJ; the articular disc functions as the 3rd bone in the TMJ.

Why? Exhibits 2 types of movement
- ginglymoid = hinging/rotational movement
- arthrodial = gliding/translational movement

TMJ formed by: 
1. **C**ondyle (Mandible)
2. **M**andibular Fossa (*Temporal bone*) (i.e. glenoid fossa/articular fossa) e
3. **A**rticular disc - Separates the **C**ondyle and **M**andibular fossa

## Articular Disc
- composition: dense fibrous CT, relatively avascular and aneural
- Shape: 
	- Sagitally, can be divided into 3 regions according to thickness
		- Intermediate zone:  the thinnest part of the disc, located centrally
		- Anterior & Posterior Regions are thicker
		- Posterior region is the thickest
	- Anterior View: disk is thicker medially than laterally
	- Disc is flexible and adapatable but the morphology is not altered during function; only if there are destructive forces or joint pathology.

## Retrodiscal Tissue
- anteriorly, attaches to the posterior region of the  **Articular Disc 
- highly vascularized and innervated
-  *superior retrodiscal lamina*
	- attaches the posterior aspect of the articular **disc to** the **tympanic plate**
	- contains many elastic fibers; **ELASTIC** - stretches
	- ***the retraction force of the superior retrodiscal lamina holds the disc rotated as far posteriorly on the condyle as the width of the articular disc space will permit.***
		- this retractive force is only present during wide opening movements
- *inferior retrodiscal lamina*
	- attaches the inferior border of the posterior edge of the **disc to** the posterior margin of the articular surface of the **condyle**
	- contains many collagenous fibers; **COLLAGENOUS** - does not stretch
- posterior aspect
	- attached to a large venous plexus, which fills with blood as the condyle moves forward

## Capsular Ligament
- surrounds most of the joint
- attached to the superior and inferior regions and medial and lateral regions of the centrally-located disc
- Attached superiorly to the anterior margin of the articular surface of the temporal bone by collagenous fibers
- Attached inferiorly to the anterior margin of the articular surface of the condyle by collagenous fibers
- The corresponding attachments form superior and inferior cavities
- the inner surface of the cavities are formed by specialized endothelial cells that form a synovial lining.
- the **synovial fringe**, located at the anterior border of the retrodiscal tissues, and the specialized endothelial cells produce synovial fluid that fills the superior and inferior cavities.
- thus, the TMJ is considered a synovial joint
- Synovial fluid
	- 1)  Serves as a medium for metabolic requirements between the vessels of the capsule and the articular tissues, since the articular surfaces of the TMJ are nonvascular.
	- 2) lubrication
		- boundary - primary mechanism of joint lubrication; synovial fluid is forced from one cavity to another during movement; lubrication during movement
		- weeping - articular tissue surfaces absorb the synovial fluid; this is how metabolic exchange takes place, since the joint is relatively avascular; lubrication during compression

#### 4 Histologic Layers/zones
from superficial to deep:
10) Articular zone
	1) made of dense fibrous CT 
		1) this is opposed to most synovial joints, where the articular zone is made of hyaline cartilage
11) Proliferative zone
	1) contents: undifferentiated mesenchymal tissue, which stimulated to differentiate and proliferate into articular cartilage during loading.
12) fibrocartilaginous zone
	1) contents: collagen fibrils arranged in bundles in a crossing pattern in random orientation to resist compressive and lateral forces
13) calcified cartilage zone
	1) contents: chondrocytes and chondroblasts; the dead chondrocytes turn into bone cells within the medullary cavity
	2) the scaffolding provided by the extracellular matrix provides an active site for remodeling activity during endosteal bone growth

ARTICULAR CARTILAGE
- components: chondrocytes + intercellular matrix
- chondrocytes produce the intercellular matrix, composed of collagen, proteoglycans, glycoproteins, and enzymes

# Innervation of TMJ
- trigeminal nerve
- mandibular nerve branches - afferent
- mostly provided by: auriculotemporal nerve
- deep temporal and masseteric nerves provide additional innervation

# Vasculature of TMJ
- Posteriorly: superficial temporal artery
- Anteriorly: Middle Meningeal artery
- Inferiorly: Internal maxillary artery
- other arteries: deep auricular, anterior tympanic, and ascending pharyngeal arteries.
- vasculature of condyle: through inferior alveolar artery and "feeder vessels" that arise from larger vessels, entering the condyle directly

# Ligaments of TMJ
- not active during joint function
- passively limit/restrict border movements, sort of like a seatbelt when it tightens.
- Elongation of a ligament due to sudden or prolonged forces compromises joint function.

## FUNCTIONAL LIGAMENTS
14. Collateral ligaments
	1. *medial discal ligament*
	2. *lateral discal ligament* ^5da91a
	- divide the joint mediolaterally into superior and inferior cavities
	- proprioreceptive feedback (richly innvervated)
	- strain on these ligaments can cause pain
	- Restricts any movements of the disc away from the condyle
		- allows disc to move passively with the condyle during movements anteriorly and posteriorly
		- allows disc to "stick" to the condyle/move with the condyle
15. capsular ligament
	- encompasses the entire TMJ, thus retaining the synovial fluid
	- attached superiorly to to the temporal bone, inferiorly to the condyle
	- resist any medial, lateral, or inferior forces
	- proprioreceptive feedback (richly innervated)
16. Temporomandibular ligament (TML)
	- reinforces the the lateral aspect of the capsular ligament, positioned correspondingly
	- rotational movement of TMJ occurs during the first 20-25mm of mouth opening (distance between upper and lower teeth)
	- the TML (oblique portion) creates the translational movement of the condyle and limits this movement as the entire condyle/disc moves forwards and downwards simultaneously, preventing the mandible from impinging on submandibular and retromandibular structures
	1. *outer oblique portion* 
		1. limits mouth opening during translation
	2. *inner horizontal portion*
		1. limits posterior movement of the condyle and disc
## ACCESSORY LIGAMENTS
17. sphenomandibular ligament
	1. does not significantly limit movement
18. stylomandibular ligament
	1. most relaxed when mandible is open
	2. most taut when the mandible is protruded

# Normal Biomechanical function of the TMJ

Follows orthopedic principles:
21. Ligaments do not actively participate in normal function

of the TMJ. They act as guidewires, restricting certain joint

movements while permitting others. They restrict joint move-

ments both mechanically and through neuromuscular reflex

activity (see Chapter 2).

22. Ligaments do not stretch. If traction force is applied, they can

become elongated, increasing in length. (Stretch implies the

ability to return to the original length.) Once ligaments have

been elongated, normal joint function is often compromised.

23. The articular surfaces of the TMJs must be maintained in con-

stant contact. This contact is produced by the muscles that

pull across the joints (the elevators: temporal, masseter, and

medial pterygoid).

# Treatment of muscle Disorders
1) cocontraction - no treatment if truma, since normal physiological response to injury; Treat the CAUSE, which can be a poorly fitting restoration or stress, or some deep pain source

# Treatment of TMDs

- do not direct treatment towards the sounds
- sounds are common in the general public
- joint sounds are not often associated with pain or even TMD dysfunction
- GOAL for disc displacement and dislocations = reduce pain; NOT to recapture the disc

# Disc dislocation with reduction:
## Anterior positioning device
- mandible is more forward
- condyle-disc relationship is reestablished
- disc is not permanently recaptured
- immediate reduction in pain, as it prevents the condyle from impinging on the retrodiscal tissues 
- allows retrodiscal tissues to repair and adapt so that when the condyle is positioned back into the fossa, there is no pain 
- disc is still anteriorly displaced
- may cause open bite due to contracture of **inferior lateral pterygoid muscle**

# Disc dislocation without reduction
- CONTRAINDICATED: anterior positioning device 
- treatment: reduce or recapture disc by manual manipulation - achievable if disc still has normal morphology (thick ant & post, thin medially); disc lock < 1 week = treatment has high chance of success

Technique for manual manipulation:
2) Relax **superior lateral pterygoid muscle**
3) increase disc space so disc can be repositioned on the condyle
	1) - increased activity of elevator muscles will increase interarticular pressure, making disc reduction more difficult
4) Condyle must be in maximal forward translatory position
- the superior retrodiscal lamina exerts a posterior/retractive force on the disc

Self-reduction: teeth slightly apart, move mandible opposite side of dislocation as far as possible then open mouth widest
