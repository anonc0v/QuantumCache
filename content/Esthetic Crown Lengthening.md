# Esthetic Crown Lengthening

## Etiology
- Tooth Fracture
- carious lesion
- gummy smile
- altered passive eruption
- altered active eruption
- irregular gingival margins
- subgingival caries
- prosthetic reasons

## Gummy Smile
- From an esthetic point of viewL :1-2mm of gingival display when smiling
- When the Px has excessive gingival display of the maxillary anterior teeth
- Tx: lip reposition (because crown lengthening will make the teeth look too long and if the contact point is too far ( more than 5mm from contact point to crest) then they'll get black triangles

## Altered Passive Eruption
- Occurs when the teeth fully erupt but the gingival tissues fail to recede to the normal apical level
- The alveolar crest has a normal relationship with the CEJ
- The only abnormality is the coronal displacement of the gingival margin position
- Resulting in excessive gingival display and short clinical crowns

Keratinized tissue; not all keratinized tissue is attached gingiva

Osteoplasty -

- but osteoplasty is touching non-supporting bone

## Technical Aspects
- used to extend the clinical crown apically
- Objectives:
	- Improve esthetics
	- Removal of sub gingival caries
	- Maintenance of restorations and crowns
	- occlusal plane correciton
	- to facilitate oral hygiene

## Hemisection
- prognosis
- best for shorter root trunks
- worst for longer root trunks
- where root trunk is measured from CEJ to Furcation

## Surgical Considerations
- importance of the tooth in the arch
- extension of the subgingival caries
- apical extension of the fracture
- root shape and size
- bone status (periodontal)
- crown-root ratio
- remaining bone after surgery
- oral hygiene
- keratinized tissue - 1.5mm is enough; 
- prosthetic needs
- periodontal status of adjacent teeth

position of tooth in arch - taken into consideration in the latest classification 

>30-40% plaque index do not do surgery - headaches in impressions, surgery, etc; but literature is at ≤10% 

Primary traumatic occlusion - occurs over healthy periodontium
Secondary traumatic occlusion - occurs over a periodontium that has already been diseased (perio) or over a healthy but previously diseased periodontium

## Width of Keratinized Gingiva

biological width = dentogingival complex
- a band of keratinized tissue must remain after the surgery (with or without osseous surgery)
- min 2mm of attached gingiva
- under normal conditions the dentogingival complex measures:
	- 3mm facially
	- 4.5 - 5.0 mm interproximally 

to determine how much bone should be removed: 
5mm for future bone crest to the future finishing line
- you are losing 3mm 
- and the other 2mm for ferrule effect

# Recession
- if the Px has papilla, we can assure the Px that the papilla will remain
- if the Px doesn't have papilla, there will still be black spaces

>[!note]
> the free gingival groove should match the PPD
> the thicker the tissue, the more likely we will see the orange-peel appearance (the stubbles) on the tissues

- internal or external bevel?
- Flap design
	- partial thickness and full thickness flap
- the faster you work, the better results because the tissues blood supply will be compromised during surgery.
- release the mucosal tissues beyond the MGJ, by moving your blade mesial and distal throughout the entire flap.
- go beyond CEJ 2mm - this is when you know you have pulled the flap enough (tension free) because you'll have contraction
- the higher you go, the more chances of brusing on the face/under the eye.
- use 3-4 blades.
- if there's an abraction, the tissues will retract; that is why you can do a enameloplasty/cementoplasty
- if the depth of the abfraction is ≥1.5-2.0mm then you have to fill it in.
- Once the flap is raised, do SRP
- the CT will be more bloody, which is the area of the partial thickness; the one less bloody or white is the area of the full thickness flap.

- citric acid, tetracycline to prepare the tissues, but now it is known no need to do so.
- epithelium is nonvascular, keratinized tissue, so we have to deepithelize the papilla so that the flap will attach
- they are using sling suture... it's a type of suspensory suture
- horizontal mattress and... are also other choices so that the tissues are pulled upwards.
