# Implant Terminology
## Osseointegration 
- first described in 1952 by Branemark as direct contact between bone and implant (as opposed to soft tissue between the implant and bone)
	- definition: "a process whereby clinically asymptomatic rigid fixation of alloplastic materials is achieved, and maintained, in bone during functional loading"
	- also referred to as *secondary stability*
## Secondary stability
- stability of implant due to new bone formation
## Primary Stability
- initial level of mechanical or frictional stability in bone
- important during the healing process, as micromovements can compromise osseointegration

### Insertion Torque
- Rotational force recorded during implant insertion, measured in Newton centimeters. 
- recommended by the implant manufacturer
## Relationship between Primary and Secondary
- after initial implant placement, primary stability decreases, while secondary stability increases from new bone formation.

### Stability dip
- period between primary and secondary stability which inadequate total stability exists.

# Implants
## Types
### Screw-shaped Implants
- most common
- exhibits screw threads throughout its length

### One-piece vs Two-Piece
- 2-piece:
- a) implant body - anchors to the bone
- b) platform - connection to various instruments, components, and finally to an abutment/prosthesis

- 1-Piece:
- the abutment is part of the implant

![[implant_ one vs two piece.png | 200]]

### Small Diameter implants (SDIs) / Mini-Implants
- screw-shaped implants w/ diameters from 1.8 to 2.9 mm, lengths from 10 to 18 mm
- for thin residual ridges that cannot accomodate implants 3.0 mm or greater.
- treatment alternative to lateral ridge augmentation.

### Tapered vs Parallel design
- Parallel - pioneered by Branemark
- Tapered 
	- became the **standard**
	- improved *primary* stability due to ability to condense bone in areas of reduced bone quality
	- enhances distribution of occlusal forces to adjacent bone 
	- enhanced ability to safely position implants between adjacent tooth roots
	- less likelihood for buccal/lingual perforation
### Platform matching vs switching
- matching - same diameter from the implant collar to the portion of the abument that connects to the implant
- switching - diameter is narrower than the implant at the connection![[implant_matching switching.png | 300]]

# Bone Grafting
![[Implant_Bone Graft Material Considerations.png]]

## Types
a) Autogenous bone graft - graft from patient
b) Allogragrafts - graft from same species
c) Xenografts - graft from nonhuman source (e.g. bovine, porcine, equine)
d) Alloplastic - synthetic bone substitute (.e.g, calcium phosphate, bioactive glass)

### Ideal Bone Graft Properties
1) biocompatible
2) bioresorbable
3) osteoconductive
4) osteoinductive
5) structurally similar to bone
6) easy to use
7) cost-effective

### Classification of Bone Graft based on effect on bone healing
- osteogenic - ability of the graft to produce new bone; property found only in fresh autogenous bone and in bone marrow cells
- osteoconduction - serves as a scaffold for bone healing, where there is ingrowth of neovasculature and infiltration of osteogenic precursor cells into the graft site.  
	- found in cancellous autografts and allografts, demineralized bone matrix, HA, collagen, and calcium phosphate
- osteoinductive - induces stem cells to differentiate into mature bone cells; property due to presence of bone growth factors within or subsequently applied to the graft material. Principle osteoinductive materials: bone morphogenic proteins, demineralized bone matrix.  Autograft and allograft also have some osteoinductive properties.

## Guided bone regeneration
- uses barrier membranes (e.g. collagen, PTFE (expanded polytetrafluorethylene)
- to protect bony defects from the rapid ingrowth of soft tissue cells so that bone may develop uninhibited.

## Techniques
- Onlay grafting
- Inlay grafting - jaw is surgically separated then graft material sandwiched between the sections
- Ridge Expansion - alveolar ridge is split longitudinally and parted to allow placement of implant and/or graft material in the void
- socket-shiled technique - maintains volume and contours of hard and soft tissue by preserving the buccal part of the root and
- placing the implant lingual to it.  Graft is placed between the implant and bone.

## Sinus Augmentation
- Maxillary sinus floor augmentation (MSFA)
	- Lateral window technique
	- crestal sinus lift or sinus bump
