# Mandible as a Lever
Mandible: is a modified class 3 lever system and a class 2 lever when a bolus of food is only on one side of masticatory surface
- fulcrum is at the TMJ
- force arm is the center of the masseter
- work arm - different positions between the occlusal surfaces
- the closer the object is to the muscle force, the greater amount of work is generated

Class 3 lever (ideal)
- fulcrum located in joints that guide rotational movements of mandible
- work of class 3 lever system is distributed across occlusal surfcae of teeth in sagittal plane
- the closer the object is to muscle force, the greater amount of work is generated
- posterior teeth have greater cross-sectional area (multiple cusps and multiple roots with periodontal support) to distribute load
- work become less the further it is from force (teeth become smaller with less roots the more anterior they are)
- if interference occurs on posterior tooth, it will cause more destruction to tooth than if interference occurs on incisors

Class 2 lever
- efficiency increases due to the force arm being longer than the work arm
- opposite condyle is the fulcrum while the force remains the same
- when masticatory system works as class 2 lever, joints and teeth receive overloading forces

Ideal physiologic position: Class 3 lever
- arc of closure makes the finishing position maximum intercuspation, always in the same tooth contact.  The amount of force at the dental part is progressively less from the 2nd molars to the incisors, while the joints have a minimum load.  
- teeth and condyles fit in the same relationship

If patient opens or closes in zigzag, there's something wrong.

# Centric Relation
- a maxillomandibular relationship, independent of tooth contact, in which the condyles articulate in the anterior-superior position against the posterior slopes of the articular eminences; in this position, the mandible is restricted to a purely rotary movement; from this un-strained, physiologic, maxillomandibular relationship, the patient can make vertical, lateral or protrusive movements; it is a clinically useful, repeatable reference position

# Other Considerations

## Eliminating slide is not necessary if your TMJ is healthy but in certain cases can help relieve TMDs.

## Centric Relation vs Intercuspal position
- if <2mm between CP and ICP, as noted by a antero-superior slide of the mandible, selective grinding can most likely eliminate the slide.
## Direction of slide 
- A significant horizontal slide is challenging to correct solely within enamel. 
	- large horizontal slides are seen in flatter cusps
- Vertical slides are seen in tall cusps
	- easier to eliminate
## Position of Anterior teeth
- analyze the Anterior teeth after correcting "slide", as anterior guidance is important to disocclude posterior teeth during eccentric movements
- in CR, close mandible and analyze for premature contacts that prevent ICP in CR.  
## Incorrect selective grinding can worsen TMDs

Treatment Goals of Selective grinding:
1) in CR, all posterior teeth contact evenly and simultaneously between centric (functional) cusp tips and opposing surfaces.
2) Disocclusion of posterior teeth during laterotrusive movements of the mandible, guided by anterior teeth.
3) Disocclusion of posterior teeth during mandibular protrusion, guided by anterior teeth.
4) In upright head position, the posterior teeth contact more heavily than anterior teeth.


# Developing an acceptable centric relation contact position / elimination of centric relation slide

- slide is due to instability of the contacts 
- A patient's with TMDs where an unstable occlusal condition exist in CR and a slide occurs to a more stable ICP, a major goal of selective grinding is to eliminate the slide so that CR = ICP.  

## Classificaiton of CR slides:
1) anterosuperior
2) anterosuperior and to the right
3) anterosuperior and ot the left
## Anterosuperior slide
- due to contact between mesial inclines of the maxillary cusps and distal inclines of mandibular cusps

## Anterosuperior and right slide
- if the interferences are on the right side, it is due to
	- inner inclines of the maxillary lingual cusps against the inner inclines of the mandibular buccal cusp
- if the interferences are on the left side, it is due to:
	- a) the inner inclines of the maxillary buccal cusps against the outer inclindes of the mandibular buccal cusps
		- called mediotrusive interferences
	- or
	- b) the outer inclines of the maxillary lingual cusps against the inner inclines of the mandibular lingual cusps
		- called laterotrusive interferences

## Anterosuperior and left slide
- If the interferences are on the left side, it is due to:
    - Inner inclines of the maxillary lingual cusps against the inner inclines of the mandibular buccal cusps
- If the interferences are on the right side, it is due to:
    - a) The inner inclines of the maxillary buccal cusps against the outer inclines of the mandibular buccal cusps
        - Called mediotrusive interferences
