# Phases of Zirconia
- monoclinic - room temperature to 1170C
- tetragonal - > 1170C
- cubic - > 2370C
- **Adding yttria stabilizes the zirconia phase during cooling, a process known as yttria stabilization. Without yttria, zirconia would undergo a ~4–5% volumetric expansion upon cooling, leading to cracks and dimensional changes, which would compromise its suitability for dental crowns.**

# Lab processes for making Zirconica crxowns
1) Shaping - a soft- pre-sintered block; milling/ cad/cam processing is when the machine carves the block of zirconia into the crown or bridge
2) Sintering - a process where powdered materials are heated together with the zirconia to allow the particles to fuse together, improving properties (strength, translucency, durability)
3) Glazing/staining (optional)

# Classification of Zirconia
- according to ytrria content
## 3Y-PSZ 
-3Y-zirconia - 3 mol% of ytrria and 0.25 wt% alumina
- properties: high strength, very low transulcency
- for posterior
- PSZ = partially stabilized zirconia

-3Y-zirconia - 3 mol% of ytrria and 0.05 wt% alumina
- less concentration of alumina allowed better transulcency
- >70% tetragonal, <30% cubic (depends on sintering temperature)
- for posterior
- properties:
	- strongest but only suitable for nonesthetic zones to optical properties
	- high strength due to transformation toughening 
		- when a crack starts to propagate, the surrounding **tetragonal** particles partially transform to monoclinic face
		- monolithic crystals are larger in size and volume than the tetragonal crystals, creating a compressive stress around the crack that prevents propagation

## 4Y-PSZ:
4% yttria-partially stabilized zirconia (4Y-PSZ)
- balance of optical and mechanical properties
4Y-zirconia - balance esthetics and strength
- common in chairside-zirconia milling
- Chairside Zirconia

(3M, USA) and Katana STML (Kuraray Noritake, Japan)

## 5Y-PSZ 
- increase concentration of yttria to 5-6%
- aimed to compete with glass ceramics in terms of esthetics
- can be used for anterior
also considered a monolithic zirconia, but weaker than 3Y-zirconia due to its inability to undergo phase transformation (transformation toughening).
- with the increase of yttria, the cubic to tetragonal content increases to 50:50 to 70:30. 
- properties
- as yttria content increases, transulcency increases, strength decreases

# Properties of Zirconia
## Transformation toughening
- a phenomenon, where zirconia undergoes phase transformation from tetragonal to monoclinic phase when damage/trauma occurs.  This can occur during adjustment of indirect zirconia restorations before cementation to achieve proper proximal contacts, contours, and occlusion.
- effect? enhances strength and fracture toughness that prevents crack propagation
- How?  Monoclinic phase of zirconia takes up more volume than tetragonal phase, so a compression occurs around the crack as the tetragonal particles converts to monoclinic.
